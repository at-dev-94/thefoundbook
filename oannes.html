Babylonian Oannes

Oannes was one of the earliest recorded fish-men, he was also known as Ea, or Enki, one of the great Gods of the Babylonians. Ea-Oannes was depicted in symbolism as a dolphin. Enki was the Creator God Yahweh-Jehovah, first named in the Phoenician myths as Ba'al Saphon.

The Dolphin was a substitute symbol in Sumero-Semetic lands to represent the fish symbol of Ea-Oannes.

Enki who had helped man before the flood, fulfilled the function of the Hebrew Creator God Yahweh-Jehovah of the Old Testament. Enki was the original amphibious god.

Out of This World. Various Authors. Macdonald & Co. Pub. London 1989. Page 374.

The earliest merman in recorded history is the fish-tailed god Ea, more familiarly known as Oannes, one of the three great gods of the Babylonians.

Myths & Legends. Arthur Cotterell. Collins Publications 1989. Page 86.

Enki is the counterpart of the Babylonian God Ea.

Symbolic & Mythological Animals. J.C. Cooper. Aquarian/Thorsons. Page 79.

Sumero-Semetic symbolism uses the Dolphin as an alternative to the fish in representation of Ea-Oannes.

Sirius Mystery. R.K.G. Temple. Destiny Books. Vermont 1976. Page 125.

It was, in fact, Enki who assisted man before the flood came by warning the proto-Noah of the Sumerian deluge story to build his ark. He thus fulfilled the function of the special presiding deity of the Hebrews, the Jehovah of the Old Testament. How many Jews know that their god was originally amphibious?
